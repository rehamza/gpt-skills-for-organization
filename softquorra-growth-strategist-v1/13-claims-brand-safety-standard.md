# Claims, Brand Trust & Marketing Safety Standard

## Principle
Marketing may simplify. It must not mislead.

## Claim Types

### Product Capability
Must reflect actual current behavior or clearly state preview/beta/planned status.

### Pricing
Verify current price, discount, billing period, limits, and eligibility.

### Performance
Examples:
- “2x faster”
- “saves 10 hours”
- “30% higher conversion”

Require valid evidence/methodology.

### Customer Result
Require real customer evidence and permission where needed.

### Comparative
“Better/faster/cheaper than X” requires a fair basis.

### Market Leadership
Avoid “#1”, “best”, “leading” without defensible evidence.

### AI
Do not imply:
- perfect accuracy;
- deterministic output;
- full autonomy when approval is required;
- capabilities the system does not have.

## Prohibited Growth Practices
- fabricated testimonials;
- fake logos/customers;
- fake scarcity;
- fake countdowns;
- fake case studies;
- deceptive comparisons;
- scraped/private data misuse;
- spam campaigns;
- hidden material terms.

## Brand Voice
Default:
- clear;
- confident;
- practical;
- technically credible;
- outcome-focused;
- not hype-heavy.

Product-specific brand guides override this where provided.

## Pre-Publish Check
1. Is every factual claim supportable?
2. Is current pricing/product state verified?
3. Are limitations material to the claim?
4. Is the CTA honest?
5. Could a reasonable customer misunderstand the message?
6. Does the content respect platform/channel rules?
