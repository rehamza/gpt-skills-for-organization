# SoftQuorra Monetization Framework

## Purpose
Choose a business model that aligns what customers pay with the value they receive while preserving healthy delivery/operating economics.

Do not copy competitor pricing without understanding the value metric and cost structure.

---

# 1. Monetization Inputs

Capture:
- ICP;
- buyer;
- budget owner;
- problem value;
- current spend;
- usage frequency;
- value frequency;
- measurable outcome;
- alternative cost;
- switching cost;
- sales motion;
- customer support/implementation burden;
- infrastructure cost;
- AI/API variable cost.

---

# 2. Candidate Models

Evaluate only applicable models.

## Subscription
Fixed recurring price.

Good when value is ongoing and relatively stable.

## Seat-Based
Price per user/member.

Good when value scales with active users. Weak when automation replaces seats.

## Usage-Based
Price tied to units: actions, messages, records, minutes, tokens, jobs, etc.

Good when customer value/cost scales with usage. Must remain understandable.

## Credit-Based
Customer buys/receives credits consumed by costly actions.

Useful for variable AI/API workloads, but credit semantics must be intuitive.

## Transaction/Commission
Percentage or fee per transaction/outcome.

Good when platform directly enables measurable economic value.

## Freemium
Permanent free tier.

Use only when acquisition/viral/upgrade economics support free users.

## Free Trial
Time-limited/full or restricted access.

Good when time-to-value is short enough for evaluation.

## Enterprise Contract
Negotiated contract based on scale/security/support/SLAs/customization.

## One-Time / License
Appropriate for limited ongoing service cost or certain installed products.

## Hybrid
Examples:
- base subscription + usage;
- seat fee + AI credits;
- platform fee + transaction;
- subscription + implementation.

---

# 3. Value Metric

A value metric is what the price scales with.

Good value metrics:
- correlate with customer value;
- are easy to understand;
- are measurable;
- grow as customer succeeds;
- do not punish adoption unexpectedly.

Examples:
- active workspace;
- users/seats;
- contacts;
- published brands;
- automated workflows;
- qualified leads;
- AI actions/credits;
- locations;
- orders.

Avoid metrics disconnected from perceived value.

---

# 4. Willingness to Pay

Evidence:
- current spend;
- paid competitor adoption;
- signed pilot;
- pricing interviews;
- procurement/budget discussions;
- actual conversion;
- loss reasons.

Ask:
- what is the alternative cost?
- what outcome has monetary value?
- which budget pays?
- what triggers purchase?
- what triggers upgrade?
- what causes cancellation?

---

# 5. Pricing Research

For each relevant competitor capture:
- date;
- entry price;
- free/trial;
- value metric;
- usage limits;
- AI limits;
- team/seat rules;
- annual discount;
- enterprise path;
- notable overage/add-on;
- source.

Pricing changes frequently. Use current web research.

---

# 6. Unit Economics

Estimate per customer/month:

Revenue<br>
minus:
- model/AI inference;
- external APIs;
- email/SMS;
- storage;
- bandwidth;
- compute;
- payment fees;
- data providers;
- human operations/support directly tied to delivery.

= contribution before broader operating expenses.

Use ranges when uncertain.

For AI workflows model:
`monthly AI cost = actions × avg model cost/action × retries/overhead factor`

Do not use token cost alone when tools, search, storage, or human approval also cost money.

---

# 7. Packaging

Plans should separate customers based on value/need, not arbitrary feature withholding.

Potential axes:
- volume;
- number of brands/workspaces/locations;
- collaboration;
- automation;
- analytics;
- integrations;
- AI usage;
- support/SLA;
- security/admin;
- API access.

Avoid too many plans in MVP.

---

# 8. Upgrade Triggers

Examples:
- hitting usage limit;
- adding team members;
- need for automation;
- adding a brand/location;
- advanced integrations;
- API access;
- more history/storage;
- enterprise security/admin;
- higher AI capacity;
- support/SLA.

Upgrade should follow increased value.

---

# 9. Discounting

Use intentional discounts:
- annual commitment;
- launch cohort;
- design partner;
- volume;
- strategic logo.

Document:
- reason;
- duration;
- renewal price;
- impact on margin;
- whether discount creates misleading willingness-to-pay evidence.

---

# 10. B2B Services / Dedicated Teams

For service/client work evaluate:
- team composition;
- monthly capacity;
- scope certainty;
- delivery risk;
- seniority;
- support level;
- project duration;
- vendor costs;
- margin;
- change-request model.

Possible structures:
- monthly dedicated team;
- fixed-scope discovery;
- fixed-scope MVP;
- time/materials;
- milestone project;
- retainer/support;
- hybrid product + services.

Do not promise “unlimited scope” operationally without defining capacity and prioritization rules.

---

# 11. Monetization Decision Output

Provide:
- recommended model;
- payer;
- value metric;
- proposed packaging hypothesis;
- competitor benchmark;
- variable-cost model;
- main pricing risks;
- experiment to validate willingness to pay;
- what would cause the model to change.

Clearly mark hypothesis vs approved pricing.
